Advanced Planning and Resource Management Initiatives (0-10)

1. Road Hierarchy and Proper Classification of City Roads

SDG 9.1 & SDG 11.2

Agarta follows a clearly defined V1–V6 road hierarchy so that every trip – from intercity freight to a person walking to a park – has a safe and efficient path. This supports SDG 9.1 (reliable infrastructure) and SDG 11.2 (safe, affordable, and sustainable transport systems).

The hierarchy is as follows:

  • V1 – Regional Expressways
    High-speed corridors connecting Agarta to Pune, Nashik and the outer ring road. Limited access, grade-separated junctions, and dedicated freight lanes.
  • V2 – City Arterial Roads
    Major roads connecting different parts of Agarta and linking to V1. Carry high volumes of traffic, support BRT and intercity buses, and connect large commercial and industrial sectors.
  • V3 – Sector Connectors
    Roads that connect one sector to another (residential–commercial–industrial). They distribute traffic from V2 into the internal grid of each sector.
  • V4 – Internal Sector Roads
    Roads inside each sector, dividing it into blocks and mini-sectors. They primarily handle local traffic, school buses, and service vehicles.
  • V5 – Access Lanes & Service Roads
    Short roads providing access to individual plots, parking areas, loading/unloading bays, and service entrances.
  • V6 – Pedestrian and Cycle Network
    Continuous, safe, and shaded paths for walking and cycling, designed so that a person can travel from one end of the city to another without using a motor vehicle. These paths often run parallel to green belts and the riverfront.

The hierarchy allows speed control, traffic segregation, and emergency routing. It also makes it easier to apply intelligent traffic systems, priority lanes for buses, and future autonomous vehicles.

2. Sewage Network Monitoring and Maintenance System

SDG 6.3 & SDG 11.6

This system enables real-time monitoring, fault detection, and predictive maintenance of the entire underground sewage network of Agartha. It directly supports SDG 6.3 (Clean Water & Sanitation) and SDG 11.6 (Environmental Impact Reduction).

Each sewer line is equipped with level, flow-rate, and toxic-gas sensors. Data is analyzed continuously to prevent overflows, blockages, and untreated discharge into the Pravara River.

  • Real-time sewage level & pressure monitoring per sector.
  • Automatic detection of leaks and blockages.
  • Predictive maintenance using historical data trends.
  • Instant alerts to maintenance teams before system failure.
  • Emergency bypass routing during pipe failures.

Technically implemented using Segment Trees, Graph Algorithms, and Priority Queues for fast fault detection and response routing.

3. Population Forecasting and Town Planning Centre

SDG 11.3 & SDG 11.a

This centre predicts future population growth, land demand, traffic load, water consumption, power requirements, and housing needs for the next 30–50 years.

Data is collected from birth records, migration entries, housing allocations, and employment zones to generate accurate urban growth models.

  • AI-based demand forecasting for housing and transport.
  • Prevents infrastructure overload.
  • Ensures equitable public resource distribution.
  • Optimizes land use zoning decisions.

Implemented using Fenwick Trees for real-time population updates, AVL Trees for balanced data storage, and Merge Sort for demographic analysis.

4. Parcel Delivery Through Small Drones

SDG 9.1 & SDG 11.2

A fully automated drone-based parcel delivery network ensures fast, eco-friendly last-mile logistics across all zones of Agartha.

Drones operate between micro-hubs placed on commercial buildings, hospitals, and logistics centers.

  • Reduces road traffic congestion.
  • No fuel emissions – electric drone fleet.
  • Medical emergency & night delivery support.
  • Fastest routing using GPS + shortest path algorithms.

The routing engine is built using Graph Algorithms, Dijkstra’s Algorithm, Hash Maps, and Priority Queues.

5. Housing and Allocation System

SDG 11.1 & SDG 9.1

This initiative supports SDG 11.1 (Access to Adequate, Safe and Affordable Housing) and SDG 9.1 (Resilient Infrastructure) by ensuring a structured, fair, and data-driven housing allocation mechanism for Agartha.

The city follows a Four-Tier Housing Model to serve different income groups while maintaining equality, accessibility, and sustainability.

  • Type-1: Affordable Housing (700–900 sq.ft) — For EWS & LIG citizens near industrial and service zones.
  • Type-2: Standard Apartments (900–1200 sq.ft) — For middle-income families inside core residential sectors.
  • Type-3: Premium Apartments (1200–2000 sq.ft) — For professionals near commercial and IT hubs.
  • Type-4: Luxury Villas & Bungalows (2000+ sq.ft) — Located near riverfront and low-density green zones.
  • Each residential sector also includes internal parks, playgrounds, schools, and health centers.
  • Allocation is done digitally based on income group, family size, job location, and preference.

6. Bus Traffic and Public Transport Management

SDG 11.2 & SDG 13.2

Agarta uses a bus-first public transport strategy designed around the V1–V6 road hierarchy. This supports SDG 11.2 (safe and affordable transport) and SDG 13.2 (integration of climate measures in city planning) by reducing private vehicle dependency and emissions.

The system combines three layers of bus services:

  • Trunk BRT (Bus Rapid Transit) Routes
    Operate on V1 and V2 corridors with dedicated lanes, off-board ticketing, level boarding, and signal priority at junctions.
  • Feeder and Loop Buses
    Smaller buses running on V3 and V4 roads, connecting neighborhoods, schools, and commercial sectors to the nearest BRT station.
  • Last-Mile & On-Demand Services
    Electric mini-buses and shuttles using V4 and V5 roads for last-mile connectivity, especially for elderly and differently-abled citizens.

Smart Bus Management Platform:

  • Real-time GPS tracking of buses and live ETA display at stations and on mobile apps.
  • Dynamic scheduling based on demand: peak-hour frequency increase and off-peak optimization.
  • Priority at signals on V2 roads using adaptive traffic lights.
  • Integrated ticketing system (smart cards / UPI) common to BRT, feeders, and metro.

From a technical point of view, the system uses graphs and shortest-path algorithms to design optimal routes, queues to simulate and control bus dispatch, and priority queues (heaps) to handle delays and rescheduling.

7. City Revenue Collection and Expenditure Transparency System

SDG 16.6 & SDG 11.a

This system publicly displays how city revenue is collected and spent across infrastructure, health, transport, housing, and environment.

Revenue sources include property tax, commercial licensing, industrial tax, tourism, transport fares, and utilities.

  • Real-time financial dashboards.
  • Public audit transparency.
  • Fraud detection and anomaly tracking.
  • Budget optimization for city services.

Implemented using Red-Black Trees for ordered finance records, Hashing for instant lookup, and Heaps for top-spending department tracking.

8. House Number Allocation & Smart Addressing System

SDG 9.1 & SDG 11.3

This initiative provides a unified digital address and building identification system for the entire Agartha city to support navigation, emergency services, postal delivery, and smart governance.

The house number format follows a structured smart-code:
Format: [Direction][Sector-Type][Sector-Number][Street-Number][Building-Number]
Example: NWSR-1-10

  • NW → North-West City Zone
  • SR → Sector Residential (SC-Commercial, SI-Industrial, SP-Public)
  • 1 → Sub-sector or cross road number
  • 10 → Building or plot number
  • Integrated with GIS mapping, emergency response, drones, and smart postal services.
  • Supports automatic routing for ambulances, fire brigades, and delivery services.
